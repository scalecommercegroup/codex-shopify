# SEO content reference

Optimize for a real audience and a clear page purpose. Search visibility depends on crawlable, useful, trustworthy content and sound technical ownership; it cannot be guaranteed by a section template.

## Establish the content contract

Before writing, identify:

- primary audience and stage of decision-making;
- dominant page intent: learn, compare, browse, buy, book, contact, or visit;
- main topic or entity;
- distinct value the page provides;
- verified facts and their source;
- primary and supporting conversions;
- pages that should link to or from this content;
- language, market, and canonical URL.

Do not create a page solely because a keyword exists. Confirm that the page serves a distinct customer need and is not a duplicate or doorway variant.

## Visible page content

- State what the page offers early, using concrete language.
- Use a descriptive page H1 aligned with the primary purpose.
- Break long content into useful H2 and H3 sections that answer distinct subtopics.
- Write headings for comprehension, not as containers for exact-match phrases.
- Add specific evidence: process, materials, eligibility, comparisons, limitations, delivery, expertise, or next steps when verified and relevant.
- Keep the most important answer in visible rendered content rather than an image, tab, or hidden SEO block.
- Remove filler that repeats the same claim in different words.
- Do not target arbitrary word counts. Stop when the intent is answered completely and clearly.

## Page titles and meta descriptions

Document metadata belongs to the page layout, template, CMS fields, or Shopify resource—not an ordinary reusable section.

Page title:

- make it unique, descriptive, concise, and accurate;
- distinguish the page from similar pages;
- keep branding concise and consistent;
- avoid keyword repetition and boilerplate that overwhelms the page topic;
- align it with the visible main heading and language.

Meta description:

- summarize the specific page in natural language;
- include useful differentiators or facts that are actually present;
- keep it unique where practical;
- avoid lists of keywords and unsupported promotional claims;
- treat it as a suggestion, since search engines may use visible page content instead.

Do not promise a fixed pixel or character length. Search-result presentation varies by query and device.

## Internal links

- Link to the next logical task, deeper explanation, parent category, related service, policy, or conversion page.
- Use link text that explains the destination outside its surrounding sentence.
- Avoid many repeated links to the same destination in one section.
- Do not create links only to force keywords into anchors.
- Confirm every CTA has a real destination before rendering it.
- Preserve canonical, breadcrumb, and navigation consistency when URLs change.

## Ecommerce page guidance

### Homepage

- Explain the store's primary offer and audiences without trying to rank for every category.
- Link clearly to priority categories, services, guides, and trust pages.
- Keep repeated product rails distinct in purpose.

### Collection or category

- Use a unique H1 and concise introductory explanation above the grid.
- Keep substantial supporting guidance below the product grid when it would otherwise obstruct shopping.
- Explain category distinctions, selection criteria, compatible uses, and related guides.
- Avoid near-identical descriptions across filtered or sibling categories.
- Keep filter-generated URLs aligned with the site's indexing and canonical strategy.

### Product

- Use the product name as the page H1.
- Provide unique, accurate descriptions and specifications instead of only supplier boilerplate.
- Make price, availability, variants, shipping claims, returns, and compatibility consistent with live data.
- Add genuine reviews only from a verified source and never synthesize rating data.
- Keep related guides useful and product-relevant.

### Service

- Explain who the service is for, what is included, process, location or service area, timing, pricing basis, limitations, and next step when those facts are verified.
- Link to the appropriate contact, booking, quote, or preparation information.
- Avoid cloning the same service page for many places with only the location name changed.

### Local business and location content

- Use exact verified name, address, phone, opening hours, service area, and map details.
- Keep visible content and structured data consistent.
- Create separate location pages only when each location has distinct customer value and real operational facts.
- Do not invent local modifiers or imply a physical presence that does not exist.

### FAQ content

- Include questions customers genuinely need answered.
- Keep answers visible, accurate, concise, and consistent with policies.
- Use headings or native disclosures with meaningful question text.
- Do not add FAQs only to occupy more search-result space.
- Do not promise FAQ rich results; Google generally limits FAQ rich-result display to authoritative government and health sites.

## Images and media

- Place useful images near the content they support.
- Use descriptive filenames when assets are created, without keyword stuffing.
- Write contextual alt text for informative images and empty alt text for decorative images.
- Provide captions when they add evidence or context.
- Do not hide essential information exclusively inside an image or video.

## Localization and duplication

- Keep titles, headings, content, links, and structured data in the page's primary language.
- Translate meaning and intent; do not mechanically swap keywords.
- Use the platform's market and hreflang strategy rather than section-level tags.
- Identify canonical ownership before generating near-duplicate campaign, filter, location, or translated pages.
- Do not add `noindex`, canonical, or hreflang from a reusable content section.

## Prohibited shortcuts

- hidden text for search engines;
- lists of keyword variants;
- copied supplier or competitor content without added value;
- mass-produced pages with minimal variation;
- fake authorship, expertise, testimonials, reviews, ratings, urgency, or local presence;
- headings or alt text stuffed with terms;
- content that contradicts visible prices, stock, policies, or business details;
- claims that metadata or schema guarantees rankings or rich results.
